I. LEGAL BASIS (OSSP)
This Office Operations Manual is anchored on the following legal basesandissuances, collectively referred to as the Office Systems, Standards, andPolicies(OSSP): Republic Act No. 9184 – Government Procurement ReformAct  Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of RA 9184/12009
 Relevant COA Circulars and GPPB Resolutions
 Mindanao State University Policies and Issuances
 Other applicable laws, rules, and regulations governing public procurement
I. OFFICE MANDATE, PURPOSE, OBJECTIVES, AND GOALS
A. Office Mandate
The Procurement Management Office (PMO) is mandated to ensurethat all
procurement activities of the University are conducted in a transparent, efficient, economical, and lawful manner. B. Purpose
To support the University’s academic and administrative functions through timelyandcompliant procurement of goods, infrastructure projects, and consulting services. C. Objectives
 To ensure compliance with procurement laws and regulations
(RA9184/12009)  To promote transparency and accountability in procurement transactions.  To streamline procurement processes for efficiency and effectiveness. D. Goals
 Timely completion of procurement activities
 Strengthened internal controls and risk mitigation
 Continuous improvement of procurement systems and practices

II. PERSONNEL DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
A. Head of the Procurement Management Office (Procurement Director)
The Head of the Procurement Management Office (PMO), also referredtoasthe Procurement Director, is responsible for planning, directing, and overseeingall procurement activities of the organization to ensure efficiency, transparency, economy, and full compliance with existing procurement laws, rules, andregulations. Specifically, the Procurement Director performs the following functions:
a.1 Strategic Procurement Planning
Leads the preparation, consolidation, and implementation of theAnnual
Procurement Plan (APP) in coordination with end-user units, ensuringthatprocurement activities are aligned with institutional goals, budget allocations, and timelines. a.2 Policy Implementation and Compliance
Ensures strict compliance with applicable procurement laws, rules, andregulations (such as RA 9184/12009 and its IRR, if applicable), as well asinternal
procurement policies and guidelines. a.3 Oversight of Procurement Activities
Supervises and monitors all procurement processes, including bidding, alternative methods of procurement, contract preparation, and post-awardactivities, to ensure transparency, competitiveness, and value for money. a.4 Coordination with the Bids and Awards Committee (BAC)
Provides technical and administrative support to the BAC, facilitatesprocurement proceedings, and ensures timely submission of procurementdocuments and reports required by oversight agencies. a.5 Contract Management and Monitoring
Oversees contract implementation, monitors supplier performance, andensures that deliveries, services, and project outputs comply withapprovedspecifications, contract terms, and schedules. a.6 Risk Management and Internal Controls
5
Identifies procurement risks and implements internal control mechanismstoprevent delays, cost overruns, procurement irregularities, and audit findings. a.7. Capacity Building and Staff Development
Leads and supervises PMO personnel, ensures continuous trainingandprofessional development, and promotes ethical standards and accountabilityamong procurement staff. a.8 Stakeholder Coordination and Advisory Role
Coordinates with end-user units, finance, planning, legal offices, andexternal
suppliers to resolve procurement issues and provides expert advicetomanagement on procurement strategies and decisions. a.9 Documentation, Reporting, and Audit Support
Ensures accurate documentation, records management, andtimelysubmission of procurement-related reports; responds to audit observationsandimplements corrective actions when necessary. a.10 Continuous Improvement and Innovation
Recommends improvements in procurement systems and processes,
including the adoption of electronic procurement platforms and best practices, to enhance efficiency and transparency. a.11 Validation and Approval of Procurement Documents
No procurement document shall proceed for approval, bidding, posting, award, or contract execution without the Procurement Director’s signature as validation, except where otherwise provided by law or delegated authority. C. Procurement Canvassing Officer
The Procurement Canvassing Officer is responsible for conducting marketsurveys and price canvassing to ensure that procurement activitiesaresupported by accurate, reasonable, and competitive price information, incompliance with applicable procurement laws, rules, and internal policies. Specifically, the Procurement Canvassing Officer shall:
c.1 Conduct Price Canvassing and Market Survey
Undertake price canvassing and market research by requesting quotationsfrom qualified and legitimate suppliers to determine prevailing market pricesforgoods, services, and small-value procurements. c.2 Preparation and Issuance of RFQs
Prepare, issue, and distribute Requests for Quotation (RFQs) in accordancewith approved Purchase Requests (PRs), technical specifications, andprocurement timelines. c.3 Verification of Supplier Eligibility
Ensure that suppliers invited for canvassing are properly accredited, eligible, and not blacklisted, in accordance with procurement rules andinternal
accreditation requirements. c.4 Evaluation of Quotations
Review and evaluate quotations received for completeness, correctness, andconformity with required specifications, quantities, and terms. c.5 Price Reasonableness and Cost Comparison
Analyze and compare quoted prices to determine price reasonableness, prepare abstracts of canvass, and identify the most advantageous quotationbased on price and compliance. c.6 Documentation and Record-Keeping
Ensure proper documentation, filing, and safekeeping of canvassing records, RFQs, supplier quotations, abstracts, and related procurement documentsforaudit and reference purposes. c.7 Coordination with End-User Units

Ensure that all canvassing activities comply with applicable procurement laws, rules, and regulations, and observe transparency, fairness, and confidentialitythroughout the process. c.9 Endorsement for Further Processing

IV. PROCESS FLOW
The PMO follows a standardized procurement process, which includes:
1. Receipt of Purchase Request (PR)
Receive duly accomplished Purchase Requests (PRs) fromend-user units. 2. Verification Against PPMP/APP
Review and verify the PRs against the approved Project Procurement ManagementPlan (PPMP) and Annual Procurement Plan (APP). 3. Validation by the PMO Director
Forward PRs for review, validation, and signature of the PMODirector tocertifycompleteness and compliance. 4. Pre-Numbering and Control of PRs
Assign control numbers to validated PRs and record them in the procurement trackinglog. 5. Endorsement to Budget Management Office
Endorse controlled PRs to the Budget Management Office for fund availabilityandobligation clearance. 6. Receipt of Approved PRs
Receive approved Purchase Requests signed by the Chancellor or authorizedapproving authority. 7. Generation of Requests for Quotations (RFQs)
Generate RFQs based on the approved PRs, including complete technical
specifications and approved ABC.

8. Evaluation of Generated RFQs
Review RFQs for accuracy, completeness, and consistency with the approvedPRs. 9. Printing of RFQs / SVPs
Print three (3) or four (4) copies of RFQs or Small Value Procurement (SVP)documents, as required. 10. Posting to PhilGEPS
Post RFQs and SVPs to PhilGEPS for procurements with an ApprovedBudget forthe Contract (ABC) of:
o ₱50,000 and above for RFQs
o ₱200,000 and above for Small Value Procurement (SVP)

V. RISK MANAGEMENT AND ACTION PLAN
The Procurement Management Office (PMO) adopts a proactiveriskmanagement approach to ensure the integrity, efficiency, and complianceofprocurement activities. Potential risks are systematically identified, assessed, andaddressed through appropriate mitigation measures and continuous improvementactions to safeguard public resources and uphold procurement standards. A. Identified Risks
1. Delays in Procurement Timelines
Procurement activities may be delayed due to late submission of PurchaseRequests, incomplete requirements, coordination issues among offices, or non-adherence to procurement schedules, resulting in disruption of operationsanddelayed delivery of goods and services. 2. Incomplete or Incorrect Documentation
Errors, omissions, or inconsistencies in procurement documents suchasPRs, RFQs, AOQs, and Pos may lead to reprocessing, audit observations, anddelaysinprocurement execution. 3. Non-Compliance with Procurement Laws and Regulations
15
Failure to comply with applicable procurement laws, rules, and regulations,
including prescribed procedures and posting requirements, may exposetheinstitution to audit findings, legal risks, and reputational damage. 4. Supplier Inability to Deliver
Suppliers may fail to deliver goods or services on time, provide substandardproducts, or fail to fulfill contractual obligations, which could disrupt operationsand require alternative procurement measures. B. Risk Mitigation Measures
1. Strict Adherence to Procurement Calendars
The PMO enforces approved procurement timelines and monitors compliancewith the Annual Procurement Plan (APP) and procurement schedules toensuretimely execution of procurement activities. 2. Regular Review and Validation of Documents
All procurement documents undergo multiple levels of reviewandvalidationby designated officers to ensure completeness, accuracy, and consistencypriorto endorsement or approval. 3. Capacity-Building and Continuous Training
Continuous professional development, skills enhancement, and updatesonprocurement laws and best practices are provided to PMOpersonnel tostrengthen technical competence and compliance awareness. 4. Supplier Vetting and Performance Monitoring
Conduct pre-qualification and accreditation of suppliers, maintain asupplierdatabase, and monitor past performance to minimize the risk of non-deliveryorsubstandard products. Include contingency planning for replacement suppliersifneeded. C. Action Plan
1. Periodic Internal Audits and Assessments
Conduct regular internal audits and process assessments toidentifyweaknesses, gaps, and areas for improvement in procurement proceduresandinternal controls. 2. Updating of Procurement Guidelines and Procedures
16
Review and update procurement manuals, guidelines, and standardoperatingprocedures to reflect changes in procurement laws, policies, and organizational
needs. 3. Strengthening Coordination with End-User Units
Enhance coordination and communication with end-user units throughorientations, advisory support, and regular consultations to ensuretimelysubmission of complete and compliant procurement requirements. 4. Supplier Contingency Planning
Establish alternative supplier arrangements, monitor supplier performance, and develop corrective measures in case suppliers fail to deliver as per contract tominimize disruption to operations. VI. SCOPE

VIII. DEFINITION OF TERMS:
PPMP – Project Procurement Management Plan
A summary of the quarterly list of materials, supplies, andservices needed by all offices and colleges. It servesastheprimary basis for procurement planning and budgeting. Supplemental PPMP – An additional PPMP that may be submitted only twiceayearto accommodate unanticipated procurement requestsnotincluded in the original PPMP.
17
RFQ – Request for Quotation
A controlled procurement document used for canvassingasanalternative method of procurement (shopping), allowingtheinstitution to solicit and compare quotations fromsuppliers. SVP – Small Value Procurement
A controlled procurement document used for canvassingasanalternative method of procurement, particularly for itemsorservices with limited suppliers or when the procurement valueis below the threshold for public bidding. Acknowledgment Form – An attachment to the RFQ or SVP that servesasaformal record of all suppliers or bidderswhoparticipated in the canvassing, whether or not theysubmitted a quotation. AOQ – Abstract of Quotations
A controlled procurement document that summarizesthequotations received from different qualified suppliers or bidders.
It serves as the basis for evaluating bids andawardingcontracts. PO – Purchase Order
A controlled procurement document issued to theawardedsupplier or bidder. It is duly signed by the Budget OfficeandAccounting, and approved by the Chancellor. Re-bid – A new set of RFQs issued for re-canvassing items or serviceswhen the original procurement process did not result inanaward. Justification – An attachment supporting the awarding of items or servicesnot with the lowest calculated bid but compliant withtechnical
specifications and requirements. Withholding Tax – A tax to be recommended to the Accounting Officefor itemsor services with an awarded amount less than one thousandpesos (₱1,000). Sole Distributor Certificate – A certification that verifies the supplier or bidder asthe exclusive producer or manufacturer of thegoodsorservices being offered. PhilGEPS Certificate – A certification required from suppliers or biddersforitems or services with an approved budget abovefiftythousand pesos (₱50,000), ensuring compliancewithgovernment electronic procurement regulations.
